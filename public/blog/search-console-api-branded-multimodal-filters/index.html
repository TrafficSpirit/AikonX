Search Console API Update: Branded, Lens and Multi-Country Data

The Search Console API now lets you pull branded versus non-branded data, Google Lens and Circle to Search data, and multi-country or multi-device filters. Google announced it on the Search Central Blog on 16 November 2026. If you only read Search Console in your browser, nothing changes for you today. If a dashboard, agency report or tool pulls data from the Search Console API, these options may appear in it.

By Anisa Chong

This post sticks to what Google has published. It explains the three additions, who can use them and what to check in your own reporting.

What did Google announce on 16 November 2026?

In a post titled "New filtering and reporting capabilities in the Search Analytics API", Google's Search Console team said it had introduced several updates to the Performance report over the past year: the branded queries filter, web multimodal search reporting, and multi-country and multi-device filtering. Google wrote that it is now "bringing all three capabilities to the Search Analytics API", following requests from the ecosystem.

In plain terms, the API is the pipe that lets other software read your Search Console numbers. Reporting dashboards, agency tools and custom spreadsheets use it. Google says the aim is to make custom reporting and automated workflows easier. Its examples include querying a group of countries and device combinations in one call, and segmenting branded from non-branded traffic in a dashboard without maintaining complex keyword regex lists.

What are the three new options?

Google lists three additions.

1. Branded queries. You can group or filter data by a field called brandedClassification to compare branded and non-branded performance. Google says that, as in Search Console, this data is only available for eligible properties. For other properties, the response won't include any rows.

2. Web multimodal. You can set the search type to webMultimodal to get performance data from Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android, and image uploads in Google Search. Google says this data will also be available in BigQuery for properties with an active bulk data export.

3. Multi-country and multi-device filters. A new field called filterLists lets you filter by several countries or devices in a single request. Google's examples of country groups are EMEA, LATAM and DACH. For supported values and combinations, Google points to its Search Analytics API documentation.

Google adds that the filters work together. You could, for example, look at brand growth across specific regional hubs, or at which pages do best in multimodal search across key markets and devices.

What counts as a branded query?

Google defined this in its 20 November 2025 post, when the branded filter first arrived in the Search Console screen. A branded query includes your brand name (its example is Google), variations or misspellings of it (Gogle), and brand-related products or services (Gmail).

Google says the classification is not the regular-expression, keyword-matching method already available under "Filter by query". It says an internal, AI-assisted system makes the call. That system covers your brand name in all languages, typos, and queries that don't include the brand name but refer to a unique product or service of the site.

Google gives two cautions. First, because brand classification is dynamic and contextual, some queries may occasionally be misidentified. Second, the filter is designed to help you segment and analyse your data, and Google says it has no effect on how Google Search ranking works.

Why split the two? Google's 2025 post says branded queries typically lead to higher-ranking pages and higher click-through rates, while non-branded queries offer organic growth because they show how new users find your content without any initial intent to visit your site. For a small business, that is the difference between people who already know you and people who found you by searching for what you do.

Who can use the branded data?

Not every site. Google's 2025 post gives two reasons the filter may not appear in Search Console. It is only available for top-level properties, not URL path properties or subdomain properties. It is also only available for sites with a sufficient volume of queries and impressions for Google's signals. The 2026 post repeats that, in the API, the data is only available for eligible properties, and that other properties return no rows.

If your reporting tool shows no branded data, check those two points first: whether the property is a top-level property, and whether your site has enough query volume. An empty result from a property that isn't eligible is not a measure of how many people searched for your name.

What does web multimodal data cover?

Google describes it as performance data from Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android, and image uploads in Google Search. That is search that starts with a picture rather than typed words.

Google's post does not say this type covers AI Overviews, AI Mode or visits from chatbots, so avoid labelling it "AI search" in a report. Label it as Google does.

Google has not published figures for how much traffic this represents, so the size of the segment for your business is something to check in your own data. It may matter more for businesses where people search with a photo, such as someone snapping a part, a plant or a finished product. That is our reading, not Google's.

What should a small business do about it?

Most owners will not call the API themselves. The practical steps involve whoever builds your reports.

  1. Ask who pulls your Search Console data. If an agency or a dashboard tool does, ask whether it uses the API and whether it plans to add the branded split.
  2. Compare branded and non-branded clicks. If the branded filter appears in your Search Console Performance report, look at both. A rise in non-branded clicks is a different story from a rise in people typing your name.
  3. Treat the split as a guide. Google says some queries may be misidentified, so use it for direction, not exact accounting.
  4. Keep multimodal separate. If you report it, label it as Lens, Circle to Search and image uploads.
  5. Note the date. Write down 16 November 2026 as the day these options reached the API, so a change in a report isn't confused with a change in your search traffic.

If you are comparing numbers across tools, record how each number is defined before drawing conclusions.

What should developers and report builders check?

Before building on the new fields, check Google's Search Analytics API documentation for the supported values, operators, combinations and date ranges for brandedClassification, webMultimodal and filterLists. Google's announcement points there for the full details. If you use BigQuery, confirm that bulk data export is active for the property, since Google ties multimodal data in BigQuery to that setting.

Different tools will add these options at different times, so confirm with each vendor what its connector supports before you rely on a number.

Frequently asked questions

What changed in the Search Console API on 16 November 2026?

Google added three options to the Search Analytics API: a branded versus non-branded field called brandedClassification, a webMultimodal search type for Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android and image uploads, and a filterLists field for filtering by several countries or devices in one request.

Do I need to do anything in Search Console?

No action is required in the Search Console screen. The announcement is about the API, which dashboards and reporting tools use. If someone builds your reports, ask whether they plan to use the new options.

Why is branded data missing from my report?

Google says branded data is only available for eligible properties, and that other properties return no rows. Its 2025 post says the filter is available only for top-level properties and for sites with a sufficient volume of queries and impressions.

Does the branded filter affect my Google rankings?

No. Google says the filter is designed to help you segment and analyse your data and has no effect on how Google Search ranking works.

Does web multimodal data include AI Overviews or AI Mode?

Google's post doesn't say so. It describes the data as coming from Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android and image uploads in Google Search.
