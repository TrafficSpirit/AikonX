How to Get Your Business Cited in Google AI Overviews

AI Overviews are the written summaries Google shows for some searches, with links to pages for further reading. Nobody can guarantee your business will be linked in one. Google's documentation says no specific optimisation is required, and that a page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet to appear as a supporting link. So the work is mostly sound SEO, plus a habit of checking what these summaries say about you. This post covers what Google has published, what it hasn't, and a checklist an Australian small business can work through in an afternoon.

By Anisa Chong

What does Google say about how AI Overviews choose sources?

Google's Search Central page AI features and your website is the main documentation. Here is what matters for a small business.

How the answers are built. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use a "query fan-out" technique. That means issuing several related searches across subtopics and data sources to develop a response. While the response is generated, Google's models identify more supporting web pages, so it can show a wider and more diverse set of links than a classic search.

When they appear. Google says AI Overviews are only shown when its systems decide they add something beyond classic Search, so they often don't trigger.

What a page needs. To be eligible as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, and meet the Search technical requirements. Google says there are no additional technical requirements.

What Google doesn't say. The page doesn't explain how Google ranks one eligible page above another inside a summary. It doesn't say how many links a summary shows or how often any type of site is included. Anyone who claims to know the exact formula is guessing.

Is "AI Overviews SEO" different from normal SEO?

Mostly it is the same job. Google says you can apply the same foundational SEO best practices to AI features as to Search overall, and that specific optimisation isn't required. Its examples:

  • Allow crawling in robots.txt and in any CDN or hosting setup.
  • Make content easy to find through internal links.
  • Give users a great page experience.
  • Keep important content available as text.
  • Support the text with high-quality images and videos where they suit.
  • Make sure structured data matches the visible text on the page.
  • Keep Merchant Center and Business Profile information up to date.

Google's June 2026 post for website owners, New opportunities, control and insights for website owners, adds updated guidance on unique, non-commodity content, organising your content, page experience and high-quality images and video.

What changes is the measuring. A normal ranking is a position in a list. An AI Overview is a written answer that may or may not link to you, and it can differ with wording, location, device and day. Our guide to why AI visibility reports can disagree explains why a single snapshot proves very little. For background on the wider topic, read our plain-English GEO guide. This post is the practical, Google-specific follow-up.

How do you check whether AI Overviews cite you?

Do this before you change anything, so you have a baseline.

  1. Write 10 to 20 questions your customers really ask. Use their words and your suburb. A Melbourne dentist might test "emergency dentist Prahran Saturday" rather than "urgent dental care".
  2. Search each one on Google in a signed-out window. Use your normal location, on desktop and on your phone.
  3. Record what you see. Note the date, the device, whether an AI Overview appeared, whether it named or linked your business, and which other sites it linked.
  4. Open the linked sources. Are they business websites, directories, Business Profile listings or news sites? That shows which kinds of page appeared for that question.
  5. Repeat monthly with the same questions. A dated record is the only way to tell a real change from normal variation.

If no AI Overview appears, write that down too. Google says they often don't trigger, so a blank is normal.

Google also says sites appearing in AI features are included in your overall search traffic in Search Console, in the Performance report under the "Web" search type. Its June 2026 post says it has started rolling out Search Console insights about how pages appear in generative AI features, and a note added on 31 August 2026 says these have rolled out to all websites worldwide. Check your own property to see what you can view, and pair it with Google Analytics so you can see calls, forms and sales from search visits.

What should you fix on your own site first?

Start with the pages that earn you enquiries: service pages, the contact page and a few useful guides.

Put a direct answer at the top. State what you do, where, who it suits and how to get in touch, in the first few lines. A plumber in Footscray might write "We fix burst pipes and blocked drains across Melbourne's inner west, seven days, with a licensed plumber on every job." Only write what is true for your business. This is our advice for making a page easy to quote. Google doesn't say it decides inclusion.

Say something other pages don't. Google asks for unique, non-commodity content. Describe real jobs, prices where you can, local conditions and your own process. In our view, a page that could swap places with a competitor's, word for word, gives Google little reason to link it.

Use headings that match real questions. Keep key facts in text, not only inside images or PDFs.

Keep your details identical everywhere. Your name, address, phone, hours and services should agree across your website, your Google Business Profile and directories. Our post on brand AI Overviews in Google's right column covers reports of AI summaries appearing for brand searches. It is our own reading, not Google's, that mismatched details give a summary more room to get things wrong, and how often that happens is unknown.

Look after the page experience. Make the site load properly on a phone and the phone number easy to tap.

Use good images and video. Google recommends high-quality images and videos to support text. Photos of your own work and team are a sensible way to follow that advice, though Google doesn't say they are treated differently from other images.

Do you need schema markup, special files or a new kind of page?

Google's documentation says there are no additional technical requirements for AI features, and asks that structured data match the visible text on the page. That makes schema a way to describe a page accurately. Spend your first hours on content and business details.

Can you opt out, and should you check?

Google's June 2026 post describes a Search Console control that lets website owners decide whether their site appears in generative AI features such as AI Overviews, AI Mode or AI Overviews in Discover. Google says sites that opt out will not receive traffic or impressions from those features, and that the control won't be used as a ranking signal for search results outside them.

If you want to be cited, ask whoever manages your Search Console to confirm nobody has opted out by accident. Also check your pages for nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet or noindex. Google's AI features page names these as the controls for limiting what is shown from your pages in Search.

What doesn't work, or isn't proven?

  • Promises of guaranteed citations. Google hasn't published a selection formula, so nobody can guarantee one.
  • Near-copies of pages for every suburb. They add risk and little value. Write fewer pages that say more.
  • Treating schema markup as a trick. Google lists no extra markup requirement for AI features.
  • Reading one search as the truth. A single result is a sample. Repeat your checks.

How do you rank in ChatGPT as well?

People often ask how to rank in ChatGPT in the same breath as how to rank in AI Overviews. They are separate products from separate companies, and Google's documentation covers Google's features only, so we make no claims here about how ChatGPT chooses sources.

Our practical view is that the same habits are worth having on any platform: a direct answer, real evidence, consistent business details and a site that is easy to read. Run your question list through ChatGPT too and record those results beside your Google ones. Treat each tool as its own measurement.

Frequently asked questions

What are AI Overviews?

AI Overviews are written summaries that Google shows on some searches. They give people the gist of a topic and links to explore further.

How do I get my business into AI Overviews?

Google hasn't published a formula, so no method is guaranteed. Its documentation says a page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet, and that standard SEO fundamentals still apply. Check your own searches first, fix your key pages, then measure again.

Do I need special markup or a special file to be cited?

No. Google's documentation says there are no additional technical requirements for AI features. If you use structured data, it should match the visible text on the page.

Can I opt out of AI Overviews?

Google says a Search Console control lets site owners choose whether their site appears in its generative AI features. Sites that opt out won't receive traffic or impressions from those features.

Is ranking in ChatGPT the same as ranking in AI Overviews?

No. They are different products from different companies, and Google's documentation applies only to Google. Clear answers, checkable evidence and consistent business details are worth having everywhere. Measure each platform separately.
