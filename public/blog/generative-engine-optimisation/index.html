What Is Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)? A Plain-English Guide

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is the practice of making your content easy for AI tools such as ChatGPT and Gemini to understand, quote and recommend when they write an answer. It sits on top of good SEO and does not replace it. This guide explains what GEO means, how GEO compares with SEO and answer engine optimisation, how AI answers choose their sources, and a checklist an Australian small business can start this week.

By Anisa Chong

No one can promise that an AI tool will name your business. You can make your business clearer and easier to check, and you can measure what happens.

What is generative engine optimisation (GEO)?

The term comes from a research paper called "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari and four co-authors. It was published at the ACM SIGKDD conference in August 2024. The authors describe generative engines as search tools that use large language models to gather information from several sources and summarise it. Their concern was publishers. They wrote that content creators have "little to no control over when and how their content is displayed."

GEO is their answer to that problem. The paper presents it as a way for creators to improve their content's visibility in generative engine responses. In their own evaluation, the authors report that GEO can boost visibility by up to 40%, and that the strategies worked differently across domains. That is a research result on a test benchmark. It is not a forecast for your website.

In plain English, GEO is about a new kind of search. Someone types "Who should I call for a burst pipe in St Kilda?" and gets a written answer that names a few businesses. GEO is the work of giving an AI tool good reasons to name yours.

GEO vs SEO: how does generative engine optimisation differ?

SEO aims to place your page in a list of results. A searcher sees ten links, picks one and visits your site. GEO aims to get your business into the written answer itself. That can happen in three ways:

  • Mention: the AI names your business.
  • Citation: the AI links to your website as a source.
  • Recommendation: the AI presents you as an option worth choosing.

A business can be mentioned often and cited rarely. Our guide to AI visibility reports explains why tracking the three separately gives you a clearer picture.

Measurement also differs. A Google ranking is a position. An AI visibility report is a sample of answers produced under particular conditions, and the answers can change with prompt wording, platform, location, account and timing.

The two overlap heavily. Technical accessibility, useful content and credible information still matter for both. GEO adds a focus on how your brand and sources appear in AI-generated answers. It is not a substitute for a sound SEO foundation, so if your site is slow, thin or inconsistent, fix that first.

What is answer engine optimisation (AEO)?

Answer engine optimisation (AEO) is a close cousin. We use the label for shaping content so a search or AI tool can lift it out as the direct answer to a question. GEO is the label that came from the research world. We treat AEO, GEO and AI search optimisation as overlapping names for the same job.

The label matters less than the habits behind it:

  • Answer the question plainly near the top of the page.
  • Back the answer with evidence a reader can check.
  • Keep your business details the same everywhere they appear.

Those habits suit all three.

How do AI answers pick their sources?

Nobody outside the AI companies can see the full recipe, and anyone who claims to is guessing. What we can say comes from published research and documentation.

Answers draw on several sources. The GEO paper describes generative engines as synthesising information from multiple sources and summarising it with a language model. Your page competes to be one of those inputs.

Some engines score pages. As we covered in our post on DeepSeek's authority tiers, DeepSeek's Search API gives each result an authority tier from 0 to 4 and a rerank score from 0 to 3. The score blends relevance, authority and freshness. DeepSeek has not published how either number is calculated, so we know the ingredients but not the formula.

Some engines push back on gaming. As that post reports, DeepSeek's changelog says its engine filters out "GEO manipulation", a term it does not define. Our reading is that it means content written to win citations rather than to help a reader, such as phrase-stuffed pages or near-identical pages for dozens of suburbs. That is our interpretation, not DeepSeek's.

Answers vary. The same question can produce a different answer depending on wording, platform, location, whether the user is signed in, and the day you ask.

What does a practical GEO checklist look like?

Start with measurement, then fix the pages that matter most.

  1. Write 10 to 20 real customer questions. Use the words your customers use and include your service area. A Melbourne electrician might use "emergency electrician Brunswick after hours".
  2. Run them signed out. Test the AI tools your customers are likely to use, from your usual location. Record the date.
  3. Score each answer. Note whether you were mentioned, cited or recommended, and which competitors appeared.
  4. Put a direct answer at the top of each service page. State what you do, where you do it, who it suits and how to get in touch.
  5. Add evidence. Include real project details, qualifications and reviews from real customers. Claims a reader can check are easier to quote.
  6. Make your details match everywhere. Business name, address, phone number, services and hours should agree across your website, Google Business Profile and directories.
  7. Review your structured data. Schema markup helps describe a page. It does not guarantee rankings or AI citations.
  8. Skip the tricks. Phrase stuffing and near-duplicate suburb pages add risk and little value.
  9. Repeat the check monthly, the same way. Same questions, same tools, same settings. That gives you a baseline you can compare.

What can GEO not do?

GEO cannot guarantee a mention. AI answers change and can differ by query, location, user context and platform. It also cannot give you direct control over what an AI tool says about you. What you can do is improve the information those tools draw on, and flag inaccurate answers so they can be corrected at the source.

A visibility report shows what appeared in a sample of answers. It does not prove that an AI answer led to an enquiry or a sale. Read it alongside your normal search, analytics and enquiry data.

Frequently asked questions

What is GEO in simple terms?

GEO stands for generative engine optimisation. It is the work of making your content and business information clear enough that AI search tools can understand it, and may mention, cite or recommend you in their written answers.

Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. The two overlap. Technical accessibility, useful content and credible information matter for both. GEO adds a focus on how your brand appears in AI-generated answers, and it is not a substitute for a sound SEO foundation.

Is answer engine optimisation the same as GEO?

They overlap closely. We treat AEO and GEO as two names for shaping content so AI and search tools can use it as an answer. The work is the same: clear answers, checkable evidence and consistent business details.

Can anyone guarantee that ChatGPT will mention my business?

No. AI tools decide their own answers and sources, and those change over time. Good work can improve how clearly your business is understood, but nobody can guarantee a mention or citation.
