Google's November 2026 core update: what to check and when to measure

Google began rolling out its November 2026 core update on 10 November, and as of 19 November Google's Search Status Dashboard does not list it as complete. If your rankings or traffic moved this month, the most useful step right now is to wait, then measure properly. Google's own advice is to let the rollout finish, give it at least another week, and then compare like with like. This guide covers what Google has confirmed, what nobody outside Google knows, and a short checklist you can run while you wait.

By AIkonX19 November 2026 · Information is current as of 19 November 2026 (Australian time).

What has Google confirmed about the November 2026 core update?

Google's Search Status Dashboard lists the following:

  • The incident began at 10:06 on 10 November 2026, US Pacific time.
  • Google released the November 2026 core update and says it applies globally and to all languages.
  • The rollout may take up to two weeks to complete.

At the time of writing, that page has no entry saying the rollout has finished. Because Google says "up to two weeks", a full-length rollout would end around 24 November. That date is Search Engine Journal's estimate, not a Google commitment. It could finish sooner or later.

Search Engine Journal also reported that Google's Search Central account announced the update on LinkedIn, describing it as a regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites. Semrush, in an article dated 17 November 2026, reports that this is Google's third core update of 2026, after the March update (27 March to 8 April) and the May update (21 May to 2 June).

What do we not know yet?

Quite a lot, and it is better to say so plainly.

  • What changed. Search Engine Journal reported that neither the dashboard entry nor the LinkedIn post names any specific objective, and that there was no Search Central blog post about the update. Google has not said what the update does differently.
  • Who is affected. Google has not published which types of site moved up or down. Anyone claiming to know the "winners and losers" a week into the rollout is guessing.
  • When it ends. Google gave a maximum window, not a finish date. Check the dashboard for the completion entry.
  • How it treats AI Overviews or AI Mode. Semrush notes that Google has not specified any effect on them.

We have not seen enough verified data to say how the update affects any particular industry, and we won't guess.

Why shouldn't I judge my results yet?

During a rollout, rankings can shift from day to day while Google's systems apply the change. A dip on day four may look different by day fourteen, so a screenshot taken mid-rollout is a poor basis for a decision.

Google's core updates guidance is direct on this. It says to check the Search Status Dashboard for when the update started and when it finished, and to wait at least a week after completion before analysing your site in Search Console. Semrush gives the same advice and adds that major site changes should wait until the rollout finishes.

Google's page also says core updates are broad. They are not aimed at specific sites or individual pages, and a page that slips is not necessarily a bad page. Other pages may simply have moved ahead of it.

When should I measure, and what should I compare?

Google recommends a simple like-for-like comparison:

  1. Confirm the rollout has finished. Look for the completion entry on the Search Status Dashboard.
  2. Wait at least one full week after that date.
  3. Compare that week with the week before the update began. For this update, that baseline is the week before 10 November.

In Search Console, set the date comparison to those two matching one-week blocks. Looking at Monday to Sunday against Monday to Sunday keeps weekday patterns from muddying the result, which matters for businesses whose enquiries peak midweek.

If the rollout ends around 24 November, the "after" week would be roughly 1 to 7 December. Treat that as an example only. Use the actual completion date from the dashboard.

Allow for seasonality. Search Engine Journal and Semrush both point out that this update overlaps the end-of-year sale period for retailers, so an "after" week can include demand that the "before" week did not. If you sell products, expect some of the movement to come from shoppers, not rankings. Compare the same pages and queries, and be careful reading a total traffic figure.

What should I check in Search Console?

Start with your top pages and top queries rather than the overall line. Google's guidance suggests asking how large the movement is.

  • A small drop in position, for example from position 2 to 4. Google says drastic action isn't needed, and recommends not changing content that is already performing well.
  • A large drop, for example from position 4 to 29. This deserves a closer review.

Google also suggests looking at search types separately, so you can see whether a drop was in web search, images, video or news. A site whose images fell while web search held steady has a different question to answer.

For an Australian business, also try filtering the country to Australia. A global update can show up differently in the markets you actually serve, and that is the audience that pays your bills.

What should I check besides rankings?

Many "ranking drops" turn out to be something else. Run these checks before you assume the update is the cause.

  • Tracking. Confirm your analytics tags still fire, and that conversions are still recorded after any recent site or consent-banner changes. A tracking gap looks exactly like a traffic drop. If you aren't sure, our analytics and tracking setup service covers this.
  • Indexing and technical issues. Look in Search Console for new errors, pages dropped from the index, or recent changes to robots rules, redirects, or noindex tags. A site-wide technical fault is a separate problem from a core update, and it needs a separate fix.
  • Rank tracker noise. Semrush notes that rank trackers are filtering out "poisoned" results that Google returns to some automated requests, and suggests checking an odd swing against Search Console before acting. We explain how to tell a real drop from a fake one in Google ranking drop or poisoned SERP?.
  • Demand. Check whether people were simply searching less. Seasonal dips and one-off events show up in the query data.
  • Other changes you made. If you rebuilt pages, changed titles, or switched platforms in the same window, those are suspects too.

What should I not do while the rollout is still going?

  • Don't rewrite or delete pages in a panic. Google's guidance warns against "quick fixes", such as removing a page element because you heard it is bad for SEO. It says deleting content should be a last resort, for content that can't be improved.
  • Don't change many things at once. If you edit twenty pages this week, you won't be able to tell later which change mattered, or whether the update did anything at all.
  • Don't trust a single day's data. Wait for the finished rollout and the full comparison week.
  • Don't buy a "recovery" promise. Nobody can guarantee a ranking outcome from a core update, and Google has not published a fix for one.

What if my rankings did fall sharply?

Once the comparison is done and the drop is still large, Google points you to its self-assessment questions about whether your site as a whole offers helpful, reliable, people-first content. It suggests looking at the whole site, being honest about it, and asking someone you trust who isn't involved with the site to review the most affected pages against those questions.

Improvements can take time. Google says some changes may show within days, while it can take several months for its systems to confirm that a site is consistently producing helpful content. You don't have to wait for the next core update to see the effect of improvements, because Google also makes smaller updates that aren't announced.

Frequently asked questions

When did Google's November 2026 core update start?

Google's Search Status Dashboard says the incident began at 10:06 on 10 November 2026, US Pacific time. The update applies globally and to all languages.

Is the November 2026 core update finished?

As of 19 November 2026, Google's Search Status Dashboard shows the update's release on 10 November but no completion entry. Google says the rollout may take up to two weeks, so check the dashboard for the finish date before you start measuring.

When should I check my Search Console data?

Google recommends waiting at least a week after the rollout finishes. Then compare that week with the week before the update began, which for this update means the week before 10 November.

Should I change my website now?

Not in response to the update alone. Google advises against quick fixes, and changing lots of pages mid-rollout makes results harder to read. Check tracking, indexing and technical problems first, which are worth fixing at any time. Review content changes after the comparison.

Did the November 2026 core update target specific sites?

Google's guidance says core updates are broad and aren't aimed at specific sites or pages. Google has not said what this update changed.
