SEO for Plumbers: How to Get Found for Local Jobs

SEO for plumbers is the work of making your business show up when someone nearby searches for a plumber, and making the page they land on easy to call from. For most plumbing businesses that comes down to three things: a Google Business Profile that matches the jobs you do, a website with a page for each service and each area you genuinely cover, and a way to see which searches make the phone ring.

By Anisa Chong

This guide covers what is specific to plumbing. For the basics that apply to every trade, read our SEO for tradies guide. Nobody can promise a ranking, and nothing below is a guarantee. These are the things we would check first, and how to tell whether they are working.

How do you show up for emergency and after-hours plumbing searches?

Plumbing has a type of search most trades see less of: the urgent one. People type "emergency plumber near me", "blocked toilet Saturday" or "burst pipe Brunswick". Someone in that position is unlikely to compare five websites. They want to know you are close, open and able to pick up.

Google's Business Profile Help Center says local results are based primarily on relevance, distance and prominence, as quoted by Search Engine Journal. You can't change distance. For urgent searches you can make relevance obvious, and you can make sure a call actually gets answered.

Start with these checks:

  • Hours that tell the truth. Search Engine Journal lists keeping business hours up to date and accurate among Google's suggestions. If you take after-hours calls, say so on your profile and your website. If you don't, don't claim it.
  • A phone that works at 10pm. Ring your own number from a mobile and see where it goes. A full voicemail or a number that rings out is worth fixing before any SEO work.
  • A tap-to-call button at the top of every page. Someone in a hurry on a phone shouldn't have to hunt for the number.
  • An emergency page that says what you will do. Cover burst pipes, blocked drains, no hot water and the other urgent jobs you attend. Name the suburbs you cover for urgent calls.
  • Licence and insurance details in plain view. Someone letting a stranger into their home will look for them.

Be careful with "24/7". It is a promise, so use it only if the phone is answered around the clock.

Which Google Business Profile categories should a plumber choose?

Your primary category is how Google first understands what your business is. For most plumbers it will be "Plumber", though you should confirm the exact wording in the category picker on your own profile.

According to Local Dominator's guide to Business Profile categories, there are nearly 4,000 categories, you can't create your own, and the test is to complete the sentence "This business IS a…" rather than "This business HAS a…". The same guide says you are allowed 10 categories in total, that you don't need to fill every slot, and suggests choosing the most specific primary category and adding only two or three highly relevant extras. That is a third-party SEO site's advice, not a Google rule.

In practice for a plumbing business:

  • Primary category: the trade that brings in most of your work. Type your main service into the field and read every suggestion.
  • Additional categories: only for work you really do, if the picker offers a matching option. Skip trades you only refer on.
  • Services list: this is where specifics belong. Use the names customers use, such as hot water system repairs, blocked drains, leaking taps or gas fitting.
  • Service area: pick the suburbs you really travel to. A short, honest list is more useful than a whole state.

If you change your primary category, change one thing at a time and write down the date. We can't tell you a category change will lift your ranking, so treat it as a test and measure it. What should your service pages and suburb pages say?

If your website has a single "Services" page that lists every job in one paragraph, a customer with a leaking hot water system has to hunt for the answer. A separate page for each major job is easier to read and easier for Google to match to a search.

Service pages. Consider one page each for hot water systems, blocked drains, gas fitting, leaking taps and toilets, burst pipes, and bathroom and kitchen plumbing. Each page should answer what a customer wants to know before they call:

  • What the job involves and the signs it is time to call.
  • Whether you quote over the phone, ask for a photo or visit first.
  • Your licence number, insurance and warranty terms.
  • Photos of your own work.

Suburb pages. Publish a suburb page only if you do real work there and can say something true about it. A plumber in Melbourne's south-east might write about the drainage issues common in the older homes of one suburb, add a photo from a recent job nearby and include a review from a local customer.

Avoid near-identical pages with only the suburb name swapped. A simple test: if you removed the suburb name and the page could describe any suburb, it isn't ready. If you can write three genuinely useful suburb pages, publish three.

How do reviews work for plumbers?

Google's guidance, as quoted by Search Engine Journal, says: "More reviews and positive ratings can improve your business's local ranking." Google also suggests managing and responding to all reviews, positive and negative.

For a plumber, a review does a second job as well. It tells the next customer what you fixed. "Replaced our hot water unit the same afternoon" says more than "Great service".

You can't script what customers write, and you should never write reviews yourself or offer rewards for them. What you can do is make asking part of the job:

  • Ask before you leave, while the problem is fresh.
  • Send a text with the direct review link the same day.
  • Give someone in the office the follow-up, so it doesn't depend on one person remembering.
  • Reply to every review, including the poor ones, politely and with the facts.

There is no magic number of reviews and we won't invent one.

How do you track which searches make the phone ring?

Plumbing leads often arrive by phone, which can make form-only website reports misleading. A visitor who reads your hot water page and then rings you never fills in a form, so a form-only report suggests the page did nothing.

Call tracking addresses that. With a tracking number on your website and a separate one on your Business Profile, you can see where calls come from and whether they were answered. Our call tracking service covers the set-up.

A simple monthly review for a plumbing business looks like this:

  1. Calls from the Business Profile, compared with the month before.
  2. Calls from each key page, such as the emergency page and the hot water page.
  3. Missed calls, especially after hours.
  4. Booked jobs from those calls, which you know from your own job system.
  5. Google Search Console queries that include your services and suburb names.

Rankings tell you where you appear. Calls and booked jobs tell you whether it matters.

What else counts as plumber marketing beyond SEO?

SEO is one part of plumber marketing. Check that your site loads quickly on a phone, that your quote form is short, and that your name, address and phone number match on your site, your profile and any directories. Record referrals from builders and property managers separately so you can compare them with what search brings in. For the wider picture across trades, see our guide to marketing for tradies.

How long does SEO for plumbers take?

It depends on how competitive your suburbs are, the state of your site and profile, your reviews and how consistently the work happens each month. We would rather look at your situation than put a timeframe on it. What you can do now is fix what you control: your hours, your phone, your categories and your pages.

What should a plumber do this week?

  1. Call your business number from a mobile and test what happens after hours.
  2. Check your primary category and remove extras you don't really serve.
  3. List your services on your Business Profile in the words customers use.
  4. Write or improve one service page and one emergency page.
  5. Send a review request to your last five happy customers.
  6. Set up a way to count calls by source.

Frequently asked questions

What is SEO for plumbers?

SEO for plumbers is the work of helping Google understand what your plumbing business does and where, so it can show you to people searching for a plumber nearby. It covers your Google Business Profile, your website's service and suburb pages, your reviews and the way you measure calls.

Do plumbers need a separate page for each suburb?

Only for suburbs where you do real work and can say something true and useful. A thin page that repeats the same text with a different suburb name adds little. A few strong pages are better than many copies.

Which Google Business Profile category should a plumber use?

Most plumbers will choose "Plumber" as the primary category, then add other categories only for services they genuinely provide. Check the options in the category picker, because you can't create your own, and change one thing at a time so you can see the effect.

How many reviews does a plumber need?

There is no fixed number. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve local ranking, so the best habit is to ask every happy customer at the end of each job and reply to every review. Never offer rewards or write reviews yourself.

How do I know if SEO is working for my plumbing business?

Track calls and booked jobs, not just rankings. Use call tracking to see which pages and searches lead to calls, count missed calls, especially after hours, and compare calls from your Business Profile month to month.
