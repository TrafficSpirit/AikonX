Why AI visibility reports can disagree, and how to read them

You check whether ChatGPT mentions your business and it names you. A colleague asks the same question and gets a different list. Then a tracking tool says you appear in only a few answers. All three can be true at once. AI visibility results depend heavily on how the answers were collected. Before you change your website or your budget based on a report, it helps to know what that report measured.

By AIkonX10 November 2026

What an AI visibility report actually measures

An AI visibility report runs a set of questions, or prompts, through one or more AI search tools. It then records what comes back. That usually includes:

  • whether your brand is mentioned
  • whether your website is linked or cited as a source
  • which competitors appear
  • how your brand is described

The report is a sample of answers produced under particular conditions. It isn't a fixed ranking like a position in a traditional search result. Change the conditions and the answers can change.

Why two reports can show different results

Several things can change what an AI tool says, even when the topic is the same:

  • Prompt wording. "Best plumber in Melbourne" and "Who should I call for a burst pipe in St Kilda?" are different questions and can produce different answers.
  • Platform and mode. ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode and Bing's AI features are separate products. Some offer several modes or models, and each can behave differently.
  • Location. Answers to local or service-based questions can depend on where the search seems to come from.
  • Account and history. A signed-in user with saved preferences or past conversations may see different answers from a fresh, signed-out session.
  • Timing. AI tools are updated often, and the web sources they draw on keep changing. The same prompt can give a different answer next week.
  • Collection method. The way a tool gathers answers matters, and it's often the least visible difference. The next section explains why.

Two ways tools collect AI answers

Most AI visibility tools use one of two broad approaches.

1. Asking a model directly through its API. The tool sends prompts straight to an AI provider's developer interface and records the generated reply. This is fast and repeatable. However, the setup may not match what a person sees on the consumer website or app. Search features, sources and formatting can differ.

2. Collecting answers from the live search or AI interface. The tool gathers the answers that the public-facing product returns, including its source links. This aims to show what people actually see. The results still depend on the location, timing and settings used.

Some services specialise in the second approach. For example, Cloro's search and AI-answer API describes itself as one API for Google and leading AI search engines. It lists ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode and Bing AI Mode, and returns structured data with sources and citations for tracking visibility. It is an infrastructure tool for developers, agencies and marketing teams that build their own reporting. It doesn't improve your visibility by itself.

Neither approach is "right". They answer different questions. What matters is that your report tells you which approach it used, so you compare like with like.

Mentions, citations and recommendations are different things

Reports often combine three separate outcomes:

  • Mention: the AI names your brand somewhere in the answer.
  • Citation: the AI links to your website, or to a page about you, as a source.
  • Recommendation: the AI presents you as an option worth choosing.

A business might be mentioned often but rarely cited, or cited for information without being recommended. Tracking these separately gives you a much clearer picture of what needs work. Clearer service pages, stronger evidence and more consistent business information each affect these outcomes differently.

Questions to ask about any AI visibility report

Before you act on the numbers, check that the report answers these:

  • Which AI products and modes were checked?
  • Were answers collected through a model API or from the live interface?
  • What location or region were the searches set to?
  • Were the searches signed in or signed out?
  • Which prompts were used, and do they match how your customers actually ask?
  • When were the answers collected, and how many times was each prompt run?
  • Are mentions, citations and recommendations reported separately?
  • Can the next report be run the same way, so the results are comparable?

If a report can't answer most of these, treat its headline number with caution.

What a visibility report can't tell you

Even a well-run report has limits. It can show what appeared in a sample of answers. It can't guarantee what any individual customer will see. It doesn't prove that an AI answer led to an enquiry or a sale. And no provider can promise that an AI tool will cite or recommend your business. A report is most useful as a consistent benchmark that you repeat over time, alongside your normal search, analytics and enquiry data.

A simple way to start

You don't need a specialist tool to get a first read:

  1. Write 10 to 20 questions your customers genuinely ask, including your service area.
  2. Choose the AI tools that matter most for your market.
  3. Run each question signed out, from your usual location, and record the date.
  4. Note whether you're mentioned, cited or recommended, and which competitors appear.
  5. Repeat the same check on the same basis in a month and compare the results.

This won't be comprehensive, but it gives you a documented baseline. It also makes it easier to judge any report you're given later.

Frequently asked questions

Why does ChatGPT give me a different answer from my tracking tool?

Your own check and the tool may differ in wording, account status, location, timing, mode or collection method. Any of these can change the answer.

Is one collection method more accurate?

Each one measures something different. Interface collection aims to reflect what users see. Direct model requests are easier to repeat. A good report says which method it used.

Can AI visibility be guaranteed?

No. AI tools decide their own answers and sources, and those change over time. Work can improve how clearly your business is understood, but nobody can guarantee a mention or citation.
