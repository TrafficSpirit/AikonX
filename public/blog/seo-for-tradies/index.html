SEO for Tradies: How to Show Up When Locals Search

SEO for tradies means making sure your business appears when someone nearby searches for the job you do, such as "blocked drain Brighton" or "electrician near me". It comes down to five things: a complete Google Business Profile, a page for each service, real reviews, consistent business details across the web, and suburb pages that describe work you actually do. This guide covers each one in plain English, with Melbourne examples, and is straight about how long it takes.

By Anisa Chong

What is SEO for tradies, and why does it matter?

SEO (search engine optimisation) is the work of helping Google understand what your business does and where, so it can show you to people searching for it. For a trade business, most of those searches are local and many are urgent.

Our tradies page describes the pattern. Searches like "emergency plumber near me" or "licensed electrician Melbourne" come from people with a problem to fix now, and the businesses in the top three map results and first organic listings get most of those calls. That is why tradie SEO leans so heavily on local search. You are not trying to rank for the whole country. You are trying to show up in the suburbs you drive to.

Local SEO for tradies has two parts. The first is the map results, the block of businesses shown with a map above the regular listings. The second is the organic results below. Search Engine People's guide to local ranking factors says ranking in the map results is closely tied to ranking organically for the same search, so your website needs work as well as your profile.

How does Google decide which tradies to show locally?

Local results are commonly explained through three factors: relevance, distance and prominence.

  • Relevance: how well your Google Business Profile and website match what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how close your business is to the searcher or the area they named.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted you appear. Search Engine People links prominence to reviews, organic ranking and citations.

You can't change distance. If you work from Dandenong, you won't be the closest plumber to someone in Brunswick. You can improve relevance and prominence, which is where the rest of this guide spends its time.

How do I set up a Google Business Profile for a tradie?

A Google Business Profile for tradies is usually the first thing to fix, because it is where relevance starts. Search Engine People lists the profile areas to complete: primary and secondary categories, a description of up to 750 characters, the opening date, the website link with a tracking parameter, social profiles, hours, attributes, photos, services, products, Q&A and posts. It recommends real photos over stock images.

For a trade business, we would check these first:

  • Primary category. Pick the one that matches your main trade, such as "Plumber" or "Electrician". Add secondary categories only for services you really offer.
  • Services. List each job you do, such as hot water repairs, switchboard upgrades or gas fitting.
  • Service area. Choose the suburbs you actually travel to. A realistic list beats "all of Victoria".
  • Hours and phone number. Keep both accurate, especially after-hours numbers.
  • Photos. Use finished jobs, your van and your team.
  • Website link. Point to the most relevant page and add tracking so you can see visits from your profile.

Then check the listing regularly. Anyone can suggest edits to your business details. Search Engine Roundtable reported in September 2026 that Google may publish a suggested edit if you don't respond within four days. Our post on two Google Business Profile changes to check this week has a short checklist. If you would rather hand this over, our local SEO service covers Google Business Profile optimisation and citation cleanup.

Do I need a separate page for each service?

In most cases, yes. A single "Services" page that lists everything gives Google little to work with. A page for blocked drains, another for hot water systems and another for gas fitting lets each one match a specific search and gives the customer what they came for.

A good service page for a trade is short and practical:

  • What the job involves and when people usually need it.
  • The areas you cover for this service.
  • Your licence number and insurance, shown clearly.
  • Photos of real jobs.
  • A phone number and quote form that are easy to find on a phone.

Write the way you would explain the job to a customer at the door. For the wider picture of how pages, links and content fit together, see our SEO service.

What is the difference between suburb pages and doorway pages?

A suburb page is about work you do in a particular area. A doorway page exists only to catch a search and pass the visitor along.

StoreRocket's guide on doorway pages describes them as thin, near-duplicate pages made to rank for a search and send the visitor somewhere else, and notes that Google's spam policies name doorway abuse. Its warning signs are one template with the suburb swapped, every page sending people to the same place, and pages for areas where the business has no real presence. It says the consequences range from filtered pages to a manual action visible in Search Console.

Ask these questions about any suburb page:

  1. Would I send a real customer to this page?
  2. If I removed the suburb name, would the page still be obviously about that area?
  3. Does it describe work, people or places that are true for that suburb?
  4. Do I actually service that area?

A good suburb page for a Melbourne plumber might cover the kinds of homes and pipe problems you see in that area, show photos from jobs there, and include a review from a customer nearby. Search Engine People suggests location pages include the address, services, hours, staff and testimonials relevant to that place. If you can't write something genuinely specific, don't publish the page. One strong page for each area you work in beats thirty copies.

How many reviews do I need, and how do I get them?

There is no magic number, and we won't invent one. Search Engine People lists reviews as a prominence signal and recommends a plan to ask satisfied customers consistently. It also advises replying to all reviews, positive and negative.

A habit works better than a campaign:

  • Ask at the end of every job, while the customer is happy.
  • Send a text or email with a direct link to your Google review page.
  • Add the link to invoices and receipts.
  • Reply to every review politely and with facts.

Ask for honest reviews from real customers only.

What are local citations, and do they still matter?

A citation is a mention of your business name, address and phone number on another website, such as a directory or industry body. Search Engine People says citations act as trust signals that confirm who you are and where you are, and advises keeping information consistent and accurate across the web.

For a tradie, that means a short audit. Search your business name and phone number, list where you appear, and fix anything old, such as a previous number or retired trading name. Remove duplicates. Then keep the details on your own website identical to your profile.

How long does SEO take for a tradie?

Nobody can honestly promise a date. It depends on how competitive your trade and suburbs are, the state of your current website and profile, how many real reviews you have, and how much work goes in each month. We would rather look at your situation before putting any timeframe on it.

What you can do is measure the right things. Track calls and quote requests from search, not just rankings. Call tracking helps here, because it shows which pages and searches make the phone ring. A ranking report tells you where you appear. Calls tell you whether it matters.

Frequently asked questions

Is SEO worth it for a one-person trade business?

Often, yes, particularly for higher-value jobs where one new customer is worth a lot. A low-cost first step is to complete your Google Business Profile and ask for a review after each job. If paid search or another channel suits you better right now, we will say so.

Do I need SEO if I already run Google Ads?

They do different jobs. Ads stop when you stop paying. SEO builds pages, reviews and a profile that keep working. Many trades run both, using ads for jobs now and SEO for the longer term.

Can I do tradie SEO myself?

You can handle the foundations yourself: the profile, a page per service, review requests and consistent details. Competitive keyword work, link building and call measurement are the harder parts, and that is where many trades bring in help.

How do I know if my SEO is working?

Look at calls, quote requests and where they came from, plus your position in the map results for the suburbs you want. If those aren't moving after a sensible period of consistent work, the plan needs to change.
