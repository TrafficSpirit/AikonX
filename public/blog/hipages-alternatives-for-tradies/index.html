Hipages Alternatives for Tradies: Get Your Own Leads Instead of Renting Them

If you are looking for a hipages alternative, the short answer is this. Airtasker suits occasional, task-sized work. Your own Google Business Profile, website and search ads give you leads under your own name. Most trades do best running a marketplace and an owned source side by side, then comparing cost per booked job.

By Anisa Chong

This guide is written from the tradie's side and is fair to the platforms. They bring real work to many trades. The question is what you are paying for, and whether you own any of it.

What does hipages cost, and what do you get?

hipages' App Store listing describes an app where you view and accept job leads in your area, send quotes and invoices, and manage reviews. It also says there is no commission on jobs.

The cost sits in the membership and the credits. KINGTRADIE, an agency that sells competing services, published a review saying it checked hipages' pricing page on 10 October 2026. It lists four plans, from $139 to $649 a month plus GST. Each loads a monthly allowance of credits, from 155 to 850, which you spend to accept leads. The same review says the plans start on a six-month introductory term and then renew for twelve months at a time.

Some points worth knowing before you compare:

  • Credits per lead vary. The same review says the credits a lead costs change with trade, location, job size and demand, and can change daily, weekly or monthly.
  • Leads are shared. It reports that a lead is capped at three businesses, so you may be quoting against others.
  • Nothing is guaranteed. The plans do not promise a set number of leads or paid jobs.

These figures come from a third party. Check hipages' own pricing page and your contract for what applies today.

Is hipages worth it?

It depends on your trade, your area and your quote-to-win rate. The same platform can suit one business and not another.

It tends to suit a trade with a quiet calendar that wants job invitations without building a website first. It suits a trade with high-value jobs better than one doing small call-outs, because a lead that costs the same whether the job is $300 or $3,500 takes a far bigger bite out of the small one.

It is harder to justify as your only channel. You are paying to compete for a shared lead, and your profile and reviews live on a platform you do not control. If you stop paying, the leads stop.

How do you work out your cost per booked job?

Headline fees do not tell you much. This does:

(monthly membership + credits you spent accepting leads) ÷ jobs you actually won that month

Here it is with made-up figures. Say a month costs you $400 in membership and credits, and you win four jobs from it. That is $100 per booked job. If those four jobs were worth $300 each, the platform took a third of the revenue. If they were worth $3,500 each, it took under 3%.

Keep that number for every source of work. It is the only fair way to compare a marketplace with anything else.

Airtasker vs hipages: how do they differ for a tradie?

They charge in different ways, so they suit different work.

  • hipages charges a membership plus credits to accept each lead, whether or not you win the job.
  • Airtasker has no subscription for its standard option. KINGTRADIE's review, citing Airtasker's service-fee page, says you pay 12.5% to 20% plus GST of the task price once you are assigned a task. Confirm your rate in the app, where Airtasker shows a fee breakdown when you make an offer.

Airtasker can suit small, occasional tasks because a quiet week carries no monthly charge. hipages is built around posted jobs in your trade and postcodes, and the subscription buys that volume. Both are marketplaces, which means price comparison is part of the deal.

What are the main alternatives to hipages?

Think in two groups: other marketplaces, and lead sources you own.

Other marketplaces. Airtasker is the most obvious. Whatever you try, ask how the lead is priced, how many businesses receive it, and what leaving costs.

Lead sources you own. These build visibility under your own name:

  1. Your Google Business Profile. Most trade searches are local, so the map results matter. A complete profile with services, photos, a clear phone number and a steady flow of genuine reviews works for you around the clock. Our local SEO service covers this.
  2. Your website. One page for each main service, pages for the suburbs you actually travel to, and your licence and insurance shown clearly. Our guide to SEO for tradies walks through it.
  3. Google search ads. Ads put you in front of someone searching for a plumber or electrician today. They stop when you stop paying, so many trades run them alongside the slower work. See our guide to Google Ads for tradies.
  4. Referrals and repeat customers. Often your best leads, and free. A simple follow-up after each job keeps them coming.

A useful test for any lead source is to ask what happens when you stop paying.

Owned sources build slowly. They are not a quick fix for a quiet month. Their advantage is that the work compounds, and the pages and reviews stay yours.

How do you compare lead sources fairly?

Track where every enquiry comes from. For most trades the best leads ring rather than fill in a form. Without call tracking, those calls are invisible, and a source that makes the phone ring can look like it is failing.

A simple routine:

  1. Give each source its own tracking number or enquiry label.
  2. Log every call and quote request, and mark which become paid jobs.
  3. At month end, work out cost per booked job for each source.
  4. Move spend toward the sources that win work at a price that suits your job size.

You do not have to quit a marketplace to do this. Keep it running while owned sources build, then wind back spend as your own enquiries take over. We can run these alongside any marketplace you already use and track calls so you can compare, as part of our marketing for tradies.

Where should you start this month?

Start with what is free and quick. Check that your Google Business Profile is complete and your phone number is right. Ask your last five happy customers for a review. Work out your current cost per booked job from the platform you use. Those three steps show you where you stand before you spend more.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a hipages alternative with no subscription?

Airtasker's standard option has no subscription. KINGTRADIE's review, citing Airtasker's service-fee page, says it charges 12.5% to 20% plus GST of the task price once you are assigned. It suits smaller, occasional tasks better than a steady stream of trade leads. Check the fee in the app before you accept work.

Can I stop using hipages?

Read your own contract first. KINGTRADIE's review says plans start on a six-month introductory term and then renew for twelve months, and that fees for the rest of the term may fall due if you leave early. Ask hipages for the terms that apply to your membership and your renewal date.

Do I need a website if I use hipages or Airtasker?

You do not need one to receive marketplace leads. A website and a Google Business Profile give you a second source of work under your own name, and they keep working when you pause spend. Many trades run both.

Are Google Local Services Ads available in Australia?

Yes, for qualifying trades. Eligibility depends on your trade and location, so we check that first and tell you plainly if you do not qualify.

How long do owned lead sources take to work?

It varies by trade, suburb and competition, and nobody can promise a timeline. Search ads can produce enquiries quickly. Profile, website and review work usually builds over months. That is why many trades keep a marketplace running while it builds.
