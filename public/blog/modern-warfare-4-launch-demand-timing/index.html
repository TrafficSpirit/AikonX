What the Modern Warfare 4 launch teaches Australian businesses about timing demand

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 did not launch on one day. Activision announced it on 28 May 2026, opened campaign early access on 16 October, opened DMZ early access on 20 October and released the full game on 23 October. Your business has a calendar like that too, and most owners only plan for the last date on it.

[NEED: author name, publish date and reviewed date] · 5 minute read

We use a video game launch here because its dates are public and well documented. Most small businesses will never run a campaign on this scale, and nothing here says they should. The useful part is the structure. Each stage had a different audience and a different reason to act. Any Australian business can copy that pattern for its own busy periods, whether that is end of financial year, the first cold snap or the school term.

This article sticks to what we could read on Activision's own pages and the Xbox store listing on 30 October 2026. We found no official sales figures or player counts, and we have no Google Trends data for the game, so we state no numbers for either. Where we reason from the shape of the calendar instead of from data, we say so.

The launch in dates

Each date comes from a primary source, named in brackets.

  • 28 May 2026: Activision published its announcement of Modern Warfare 4 and gave the release date of Friday 23 October 2026 (callofduty.com announcement).
  • 15 October: Activision confirmed the game launches as a standalone release and will not be in Game Pass until Holiday 2027. The post also set campaign early access from 16 October and DMZ early access from 20 October for digital pre-orders (callofduty.com COD Intel post).
  • 16 October: Campaign early access went live for players who digitally pre-ordered or pre-purchased the Standard Digital or Vault Edition (callofduty.com early access post).
  • 20 October: DMZ early access began (same early access post).
  • 23 October: Full launch on Xbox Series X|S, PlayStation 5, PC and Nintendo Switch 2. It is not on PlayStation 4 or Xbox One (callofduty.com announcement). The Xbox store page lists the same release date and names Microsoft, Battle.net and Steam as PC storefronts.

The May announcement also promised a first look at DMZ on 7 June, which is one more step in the sequence. We did not check what was published on that date.

What each stage was for

Read the dates as jobs instead of dates.

The announcement gave people a name and a date to remember. Pre-orders gave the keenest buyers a reason to commit early, and the early access windows gave them something to play before everyone else. The 15 October post settled a purchase question (do I need to buy it, or will it be in Game Pass?) a week before release. Launch day then served everyone who had waited.

That reading is ours. Activision does not describe its plan in these terms on the pages we read. It does match what the pages show: separate benefits, separate dates and a different message at each step.

Why this matters for search

We cannot show you a search curve for this launch. Reasoning from the schedule, a named announcement gives people something to look up, and each later milestone gives them a reason to look again. You can test that for any product in Google Trends. Three practical points follow.

Branded search is where earlier effort pays off. Someone who sees an ad, a referral or a mention often types your business name into Google afterwards. Search for your own name and check what appears: your Google Business Profile, your site title and your top ad. A missing or wrong result costs you people who already chose you. For local businesses, our local SEO work starts with the profile. Our SEO team can also review your site titles and key pages.

Many spikes are predictable. Activision picked its dates months ahead, and your calendar has fixed points as well. Accountants see one around the end of the financial year. Heating and hot water businesses see one when the weather turns. Our tradies page makes the same point about roofers and HVAC: seasonal demand needs pages and profiles that are ready before the rush. We cannot say how long Google will take to process new pages, so start early.

People ask AI assistants the questions they used to type into Google. When a buyer asks an assistant about a product or a local service, the answer often names a handful of brands. Whether yours is one of them depends on how clearly your pages and third-party mentions describe you. Nobody can control that directly and we do not promise it. Our AI search optimisation work starts with a test of the questions your buyers ask and a record of where you appear today. Our recent post on DeepSeek's search API shows what one AI search product reveals about how it rates pages.

Paid search and paid social during a spike

A staged launch uses awareness channels and capture channels together. Awareness makes people care, and capture meets them when they go looking. A small business can run the same split on a smaller scale.

  • Paid social builds awareness in the weeks before a known spike. It works best with fresh creative and a defined audience. See our paid social service.
  • Paid search captures people who then go looking. Set budgets and bids for the peak weeks in advance, because competitors tend to raise theirs then. See Google and Microsoft Ads.
  • Remarketing brings back visitors who looked and did not enquire.
  • Tracking has to work before any of this starts. If calls and form fills are not counted, you cannot tell which channel earned the lead. Call tracking covers the calls that analytics cannot see.

We have no AIkonX client result for a seasonal campaign to include here, so this post makes no claim about results.

The takeaway for Australian small businesses

A big launch is a staged demand calendar, and your business should plan its own spikes the same way.

  1. List your demand dates. Write down the three or four times each year when enquiries rise.
  2. Work back from each date. Fix pages and profiles first, then build awareness, then raise search budget for the peak.
  3. Check your own name. Look at what appears in Google, on the map and in an AI assistant when someone searches for your business.
  4. Set up measurement early. Install conversion and call tracking before the spike so you can compare it with the next one.

We cannot promise rankings, leads or any specific outcome from these steps. Results depend on your market, your competitors and your budget.
