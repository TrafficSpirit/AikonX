What the Modern Warfare 4 launch teaches Australian businesses about timing demand

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 did not launch on one day. It reached players in five steps over five months, and each step had its own audience and its own message. Your business has a demand calendar like that too. Most owners only plan for the last date on it.

By AIkonX Team30 October 2026 · 6 minute read

Activision announced Modern Warfare 4 on 28 May 2026 and released it on Friday 23 October. In between, it opened two separate early access windows and settled a big buying question a week before launch. By the time most players could buy the game, the keenest fans had already been playing it for a week.

Most Australian small businesses will never run a launch on this scale, and they do not need to. What is worth copying is the structure. Every busy period your business has, whether it is end of financial year, the first cold snap or the start of school term, can be planned the same way: in stages, with a different job for each stage.

The launch in five dates

Here is how the launch played out, taken from Activision's own announcements and the Xbox store listing.

Every stage had a different job

Look at those dates as jobs rather than dates and the plan becomes clear. The announcement created something to remember and search for. Pre-orders gave the keenest buyers a reason to commit early. The early access windows gave those buyers something to play and talk about before everyone else. The 15 October post answered the one question that stops people buying (will it be in Game Pass?) a week before release, which turned fence-sitters into buyers. Launch day then caught everyone else.

Activision does not describe its plan in these words. That reading is ours. But the pattern is plain from what it published: separate benefits, separate dates and a different message at each step. That is exactly the kind of plan a small business can run around its own peak periods.

What this means for search

Each announcement in a staged launch gives people a fresh reason to search. The reveal sends people looking for the name. Early access sends them looking for how to get in. Launch day sends them looking to buy. Your own peaks work the same way, just with different words. Before winter, people search for heater servicing. In June, they search for tax agents. You can see the shape of your own year in Google Trends in a few minutes.

Three points follow for your business.

Branded search is where earlier effort pays off. Someone who sees your ad, hears about you from a friend or spots your van will often type your business name into Google afterwards. Search for your own name and check what comes up: your Google Business Profile, your website title and your top ad. A missing or wrong result loses you people who had already chosen you. Most demand spikes are predictable. Activision fixed its dates months ahead, and your calendar has fixed points too. Accountants get busy at the end of the financial year. Heating and hot water businesses get busy when the weather turns. Our tradies page makes the same point for roofers and HVAC businesses: seasonal demand needs pages and profiles that are ready before the rush. Google can take weeks to pick up new or changed pages, so start well ahead.

People now ask AI assistants what they used to type into Google. When a buyer asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Mode about a product or a local service, the answer often names just a handful of businesses. Whether yours is one of them depends on how clearly your website and other sites describe what you do and where. Nobody can guarantee a mention, but you can find out where you stand. Paid search and paid social around a spike

A staged launch uses two kinds of channel together. Awareness channels make people care. Capture channels meet them when they go looking. A small business can run the same split on a much smaller budget.

  • Paid social builds awareness in the weeks before a known spike. It works best with fresh creative and a clearly defined audience. See our paid social service.
  • Paid search captures demand when people go looking. Set budgets and bids for the peak weeks in advance, because competitors tend to raise theirs at the same time. See Google and Microsoft Ads.
  • Remarketing brings people back who visited your site but did not enquire, which matters most in the days right before a peak.
  • Tracking has to work first. If calls and form fills are not counted, you cannot tell which channel earned the lead. Call tracking covers the phone calls analytics cannot see.

Build your own launch calendar

A big launch is really a staged demand calendar. Plan your own busy periods the same way.

  1. List your demand dates. Write down the three or four times each year when enquiries rise.
  2. Work back from each date. Fix pages and profiles first, then build awareness, then raise search budgets for the peak.
  3. Find your "Game Pass question". Work out what makes people hesitate, such as price, availability or booking times, and answer it on your site before the rush.
  4. Check your own name. Look at what appears in Google, on Maps and in an AI assistant when someone searches for your business.
  5. Set up measurement early. Install conversion and call tracking before the spike, so you can compare this year’s peak with the next one.

Results always depend on your market, your competitors and your budget, so no one can promise you rankings or a set number of leads. What a plan does guarantee is that you are ready when the demand arrives, rather than reacting once it has passed.
