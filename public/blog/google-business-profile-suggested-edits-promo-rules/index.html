Two Google Business Profile changes to check this week

Two changes to Google Business Profile rules were reported in the past few weeks. First, Google says you have 4 days to accept or reject some suggested edits to your listing before it may publish them. Second, Google's policy now says the listing itself should not carry time-bound promotions, pricing or direct calls to action. This post explains both and gives you a checklist to run through your own profile.

By AIkonX9 November 2026

Change 1: you may have 4 days to respond to a suggested edit

Anyone can suggest a change to your business information on Google. Search Engine Roundtable reported on 23 September 2026 that Google updated its help document to add a section called "Understand notifications about suggested edits".

According to that report, the section says:

  • If a suggested edit requires your review, you may receive an email or an in-product notification.
  • Once notified, you have 4 days to accept or reject the suggestion.
  • If you don't respond within 4 days, Google may automatically publish the update if it is supported by other publicly available information, such as your business website.

The same report says Google also wrote that it may apply suggested user edits without prior review, in cases where the edits must meet policy guidelines. In plain terms, you might not get a notification for every change.

Why it matters for an Australian local business

If your phone number, opening hours or service details change on your listing, customers see the new version. A tradie who is closed on weekends or a clinic that has moved rooms can lose enquiries when a listing shows the wrong details. Because Google says it may publish an edit that your website supports, and may apply some edits without telling you, it makes sense to check your listing regularly rather than wait for an alert. That second point is our reasoning, not a statement from Google.

What to do

  1. Turn on notifications. Check the notification settings for your profile so email and in-product alerts reach you.
  2. Check the owner email. Make sure the email address tied to the profile is one a person reads. A shared inbox that nobody opens defeats the 4-day window.
  3. Look for pending edits. Open your profile and review any suggested changes waiting for a decision. Accept or reject each one.
  4. Keep website details current. Google says it may publish an edit when public information such as your website supports it. Keep your address, phone number and hours on your website the same as your profile.
  5. Review the listing itself once a week. Because not every edit may trigger a notification, a quick look at name, hours, phone and website is worth the time.

Change 2: no time-bound promotions, pricing or calls to action in the listing

Relevant Audience reported on 8 October 2026 that Google updated the "Advertising & solicitation" section of its Maps and Business Profile prohibited and restricted content policy. It also reported that Search Engine Roundtable covered the change on 6 October 2026, after before-and-after screenshots of the policy page were shared on LinkedIn.

As Relevant Audience reports the policy:

  • In the listing: promotional or commercial content that contains time-bound promotions, pricing or direct calls to action is prohibited.
  • In Posts: email addresses, phone numbers, social media links and links to other websites that are not related to your business are prohibited. Offers and updates that may be seen as promotional are otherwise permitted.
  • The principle: the policy says contributions should reflect genuine experiences or define a business's permanent identity.

Relevant Audience also notes what the sources leave out. Google gave no enforcement date and no number of affected listings, and it did not say whether existing listing content will be removed automatically. It also says Google's page does not list which listing fields the rule covers, so treating the business description as part of the listing is a reading of the policy and not Google's wording.

Why it matters for an Australian local business

Many local businesses put a current special, a price or a "call now" line into their profile text. Under the rule as reported, that kind of wording belongs in a Post, not in the listing. The reports don't say the rule differs by country. We don't know how or whether Google will act on existing listings, so a short review now is a sensible precaution.

On pricing, Relevant Audience says the policy lists pricing as prohibited in the listing but doesn't say whether a standing service price is treated like a discount. Review each price on its merits and keep a dated record of what you changed.

What to do this week

  • Turn on Business Profile notifications and confirm the owner email is monitored.
  • Open your profile and review any pending suggested edits.
  • Compare your listing's address, phone number and hours with your website. Fix any mismatch.
  • Read your business name, description and other text fields for time-limited offers, prices and calls to action.
  • Move any current offer into a Post.
  • Check your recent Posts for phone numbers, email addresses, social links and unrelated website links.
  • Write down what you changed and the date.
  • Set a weekly reminder to look at your listing.

Questions

How long do I have to respond to a suggested edit?

Search Engine Roundtable reported on 23 September 2026 that Google's help document says that, once notified, you have 4 days to accept or reject a suggestion.

What happens if I don't respond?

Per the same report, Google may automatically publish the update if it is supported by other publicly available information, such as your business website.

Will Google always tell me about an edit?

No. The report says Google may apply suggested user edits without prior review, in cases where edits must meet policy guidelines.

Can I still run offers on my profile?

Relevant Audience reports that offers and updates are otherwise permitted in Posts, while time-bound promotions, pricing and direct calls to action are prohibited in the listing itself.

When will Google enforce the promotions rule?

Relevant Audience reports that Google gave no enforcement date and did not say whether existing listing content will be removed automatically.
