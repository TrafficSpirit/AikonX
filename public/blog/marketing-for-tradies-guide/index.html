Marketing for tradies: a practical guide to getting more local jobs

Most tradie marketing comes down to one job: when someone near you searches for a plumber, sparky, concreter or roofer, your business is the one they find, trust and call. This guide shows where that work comes from, what to fix first, how to count the calls you get, and which mistakes waste money. You don't need to be a marketer to follow it.

By AIkonX6 November 2026

Where tradies get jobs

Work reaches a trade business through a few main channels. Each needs a different kind of effort, so it helps to know what each one does before you spend on any of them.

Google search and the map pack

Someone with a leaking tap or a dead switchboard types a search like "emergency plumber near me" or "electrician Brunswick". Google shows a map with a short list of local businesses, then the regular website results. Getting into that map list, often called the map pack, is the heart of local marketing for most trades.

Google says local results are based primarily on relevance, distance and prominence, and that a combination of the three helps it find the best match for a search (as quoted by Search Engine Journal, citing Google's Business Profile Help Center). Relevance is how closely your listing matches the search. Distance is how close you are to the searcher. Prominence is how well known you are, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve a business's local ranking.

You can't change how far away a customer is. You can change how complete your listing is and how many good reviews you have.

Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that appears in the map and on search results with your name, phone number, hours and reviews. Search Engine Journal reports that Google suggests you:

  • complete your business details
  • verify your business
  • keep your opening hours up to date
  • reply to every review, good and bad
  • add photos of your work

This is the cheapest place to start, because it costs you time rather than ad spend.

Reviews

Reviews count twice. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve your local ranking, and a new customer comparing three plumbers can read the stars and a few comments. The simplest habit is to ask every happy customer for a review at the end of the job, while you are still on site, and to reply to each one.

Word of mouth and repeat work

Referrals from past customers, builders, real estate agents and neighbours are worth protecting. Marketing doesn't replace them. It backs them up, because a person who hears your name may look you up on Google before they call. If your profile is thin or your website is out of date, that person may choose someone else.

Google Ads

Google Ads can put your business in front of people searching for the services you choose. Paid search can produce leads within days of launch, and ads stop the moment you stop paying. That makes them useful for filling a quiet fortnight while slower work builds. Our Google Ads management service covers setup and ongoing management.

Social media

Facebook and Instagram are good for showing finished jobs and staying visible to past customers and neighbours. For most trades they work as a portfolio and a reminder. Urgent jobs such as burst pipes and blocked drains are often searched on a phone, in a hurry, so social comes after the first items in the order below.

A simple order to do things in

If you are starting from very little, work down this list. Each step makes the next one pay off.

  1. Claim and finish your Google Business Profile. Add your trade as the category, your service areas, hours, phone number and photos of real jobs.
  2. Ask for reviews after every job. Make it a habit, not a campaign.
  3. Make sure your website answers the basics. What you do, where you work, your licence number and a phone number people can tap.
  4. Add one page for each main service. More on this below.
  5. Set up call and quote tracking. You can't improve what you can't count.
  6. Add Google Ads if you need jobs sooner. Ads fill quiet weeks while the slower work builds.
  7. Keep going. Local marketing builds over months.

On timing, our tradie SEO service page says Google Business Profile and citation fixes often produce map pack movement within 4 to 8 weeks, and that organic rankings for competitive service terms typically build over 3 to 6 months. Those are AIkonX's typical ranges, not promises. Your suburb, trade and competitors change them.

SEO for tradies: the basics that matter

SEO means making your website and profile easy for Google to match to what people search for. For a trade business, a few basics do most of the work. If you want a team to handle all of it, our SEO for tradies service covers it and starts with a free review.

One page per service. A single page called "Services" that lists everything does little for any one search. Blocked drains, hot water systems, switchboard upgrades and bathroom renovations each deserve their own page that says what the job involves, where you do it and how to book.

Pages for the suburbs you work in. If you travel to ten suburbs, show Google and customers that you do. Each suburb page should say something real about the work you do there, instead of repeating the same text with a new place name.

Licence and trust signals. Put your licence number, insurance, guarantees and the associations you belong to where a new customer can see them. Someone letting a stranger into their home wants to know you are legitimate before they call.

Real job photos. Photos of your own finished work beat stock images. For concreters, painters, landscapers and builders in particular, a gallery of completed jobs is often the strongest proof on the page. Add a short caption with the job type and suburb.

Consistent business details. Keep your business name, address and phone number the same on your website, your Google profile and local directories. Our local SEO service includes cleaning up these listings.

Track the calls and quote requests you get

Rankings and website visits are not the goal. Booked jobs are. At minimum, keep a simple tally every week:

  • how many calls came in
  • how many were new customers
  • how many turned into quotes
  • how many quotes turned into work
  • where each one said they found you

Ask every new caller "How did you hear about us?" and write the answer down. Then move to proper tracking: separate phone numbers for your website and your ads, and a tracked quote form. Our call tracking service sets this up so you can see which channel is making the phone ring.

Also answer your phone. A caller with a burst pipe who gets no answer can ring the next business on the list.

How AI assistants recommend local trades

AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot answer questions directly, and often name just a handful of businesses. A customer might ask, "who is a good electrician in Footscray?" Our AI search optimisation page describes the signals involved as clear pages that answer real questions, structured data, and consistent mentions, profiles and citations across the sources AI models learn from.

For a tradie, that translates into things you are already doing for Google: a complete profile, consistent business details, clear service and suburb pages, and genuine reviews. Nobody can directly control what an AI assistant says about you, and anyone who promises to is guessing. What you can do is make the information about your business accurate and easy to quote. Common marketing mistakes tradies make

  • Skipping the Google Business Profile. It is free and it is often the first thing a customer sees.
  • Paying for ads without tracking calls. You can't tell whether the spend is working.
  • One page for all your services. Google and customers can't tell which job you are best at.
  • Copying the same suburb page ten times. Thin pages give nobody a reason to choose you.
  • Never replying to reviews. A reply shows a future customer how you handle problems.
  • Using stock photos. Customers want to see your work.
  • Judging marketing after two weeks. Ads can move in days, but profile and website work builds over months.
  • Buying a service nobody can explain. If a provider can't tell you what they will do and how you will see the result, keep looking.

Questions tradies ask

How much does marketing cost for a tradie?

It depends on the mix. A growing trade business covering several suburbs or service types typically invests $1,500 to $3,000 per month. Ad spend is separate and you set the budget.

What should I do first?

Finish your Google Business Profile and start asking for reviews. Both are free and both feed the map results.

Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

Yes. Your profile gets people to your door, and your website gives them the proof to call: your services, your licence, your photos and the suburbs you cover.

Do I still need SEO if I run Google Ads?

Yes, and the two work well together. Ads stop the moment you stop paying. SEO builds an asset that keeps bringing in calls and quote requests. Many tradies run both.

Is marketing worth it for a one-person trade business?

Often, particularly for higher-value jobs where one new customer can be worth thousands of dollars. If another channel suits you better right now, we will say so.
