What is vibe marketing? A plain-English guide for Australian SMEs

What is vibe marketing? In the sense most marketers now use, it is prompt-led, AI-assisted marketing: you describe what you want in everyday language and AI tools help with research, drafts, creative and workflows, while people set the direction and check the result. This guide explains the idea, shows what it could look like for a small business, and is straight about the risks, including privacy, misleading claims and measurement.

By Brian H.

What is vibe marketing?

Vibe marketing, in this guide, means directing AI tools with natural-language prompts to help produce and run marketing. MarTech's "Vibe Marketing manifesto" by Marc Sirkin defines it as machine-accelerated creativity, and describes it as conceptually similar to vibe coding. It combines human creativity with AI to speed up thinking, strategy and execution across marketing, sales and operations.

In practice, that usually means a person writes a brief, an AI tool produces a first version, and the person edits, rejects or refines it.

There is a second meaning you may run into. Some people use "vibe" to describe a brand's mood or aesthetic, such as a cafe with a relaxed, retro feel. That is a branding idea. This article is about the AI-assisted meaning only.

Where did the term come from?

The label borrows from "vibe coding", where someone describes software in plain language and an AI tool writes the code. Marketers have applied the same idea to campaigns: describe the outcome, let the tools draft, then review.

We can't point to a single person who invented the marketing version, and we won't credit anyone with it. What we can say is that MarTech published Sirkin's manifesto as a written definition of the idea and tied it to vibe coding. Treat it as one published view, not a settled standard.

What does vibe marketing look like in practice?

Here is a hypothetical example. It is not a client story and none of the numbers are real, because it has no numbers.

Imagine a small Melbourne bathroom renovation business that wants more quote requests.

  1. Research. The owner asks an AI tool to summarise the questions customers typically ask before a renovation, then checks those against real enquiries from the inbox.
  2. Copy. The owner supplies approved facts, such as service areas, licence details and warranty terms, and asks for three draft versions of a landing page headline and intro.
  3. Creative. The tool suggests ad concepts and image ideas. The owner rejects the ones that look generic or imply something the business does not do.
  4. Landing page. A draft page is assembled from the approved copy, then a person tests the form on a phone.
  5. Follow-up. An automated reply confirms each enquiry and asks a few qualifying questions. A person reads the answers and calls back.

Notice what the AI did: it produced drafts quickly. Notice what people did: chose the goal, supplied the facts, rejected weak ideas and tested everything that touches a customer.

What could Australian SMEs gain?

Used carefully, AI-assisted workflows could offer a few practical benefits:

  • Faster first drafts. A blank page becomes an editable draft in minutes rather than hours.
  • Potentially lower production costs. If a task takes less time, it may cost less.
  • Easier experimentation. It is cheaper to try several headlines or angles before committing budget.

We say "could" and "potentially" on purpose. Tool subscriptions cost money. Someone has to write good prompts, check facts, fix tone and rework weak output. Those hours can cancel out the saving. We haven't measured savings for any business, and we're not going to quote a figure. Measure your own before you assume them.

What are the risks and limits?

AI tools make fast work easy. They don't make it correct. These are the risks we would plan for.

Inconsistent brand voice. Without examples and rules, tools tend to write in a generic voice that sounds like everyone else.

Invented facts. AI tools can state things that are wrong with total confidence. Every claim about your products, prices, results and credentials needs a human check.

Misleading claims. The ACCC's Advertising and selling guide says a business must be able to prove any claim it advertises, and that claims should be true, accurate and based on reasonable grounds. It also lists making promises you can't keep, or predictions without solid evidence, among conduct that can mislead. If a claim goes out under your name, you are responsible for it, whoever or whatever drafted it.

Privacy. The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) has guidance on privacy and the use of commercially available AI products. As a matter of best practice, it recommends that organisations do not enter personal information, and particularly sensitive information, into publicly available AI chatbots and other generative AI tools. It also says organisations should consider whether using personal information with an AI system is necessary, and take a "privacy by design" approach that includes a Privacy Impact Assessment. This article is general information, not legal advice.

Broken tracking. A quickly built landing page or form can look fine and still send enquiries nowhere, or fail to record them in your analytics.

How does Google treat AI-generated content?

Google has published guidance on using generative AI content on your website. It says generative AI can be particularly useful when researching a topic and adding structure to original content. It also warns that using generative AI tools to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse.

That is not a blanket penalty on AI. The concern Google describes is volume without value. A single, carefully checked page that helps a reader is a different thing from hundreds of near-identical pages produced to catch searches. If you use AI for content, the safest approach is to publish at the pace you can review, add real expertise and facts, and keep every page useful. How can you use vibe marketing responsibly?

A sensible first experiment looks like this:

  • Pick one goal, such as more qualified enquiries from one service.
  • Write a short brief with approved facts, your tone of voice and two or three examples of copy you like.
  • Keep customer information out of public AI tools, and check a tool's terms before you use it for anything personal.
  • Review every output for accuracy, tone and claims before it goes live.
  • Test forms, phone numbers and tracking on mobile and desktop.
  • Run a limited experiment, such as one landing page and one small ad test, before you scale.

If the experiment involves content, a page that explains the service properly will usually do more than ten thin ones. Our content marketing services and landing pages work are built around that idea.

How should you measure the results?

Speed is not the goal. Enquiries and sales are. Track two groups of numbers side by side.

Production measures: the hours your team spent, the tool subscriptions, and the cost of checking and rework. These tell you whether AI actually saved effort.

Business measures: qualified enquiries, cost per enquiry and sales that came from them. These tell you whether the campaign deserves more investment.

Where does specialist judgement fit?

AI can shorten production work. It can't decide which customers to chase, whether a claim is accurate, whether the tracking is trustworthy or whether a result justifies more budget. Those are judgement calls.

Frequently asked questions

Is vibe marketing the same as AI marketing?

Not exactly. AI marketing is a broad term covering any use of AI in marketing, such as bidding tools or analytics. Vibe marketing, as MarTech frames it, is narrower: directing AI with plain-language prompts across drafting and workflows, with people steering the result. Many writers use the two terms loosely, so check how a source defines it.

Does vibe marketing replace a marketing strategy or team?

No. A prompt can't tell you who your best customers are or what you can honestly promise. Strategy, approval and accountability stay with people. AI can take on some of the production work, which may free your team for the parts that need judgement.

Can a small business try vibe marketing on a limited budget?

Yes, in a small way. Choose one goal, one service and one channel, and set a spending limit before you start. Count your own time and any subscriptions as costs, and compare the result with what you would have achieved without the tools. Stop or change course if enquiries don't improve.

Will Google penalise AI-written marketing content?

Google's guidance says generative AI can help with research and structure, but producing many pages without adding value for users may violate its scaled content abuse spam policy. The test is whether the page is accurate and useful, not whether a tool helped draft it. Review and fact-check everything before publishing.

Should I put customer information into an AI marketing tool?

Be very careful. The OAIC recommends, as best practice, not entering personal information, and particularly sensitive information, into publicly available generative AI tools. If you want to use customer data with any AI product, check the tool's terms, consider a Privacy Impact Assessment and get privacy advice for your situation.
