Brand AI Overviews have moved to Google's right column. What to check

Search Engine Roundtable reported on 2 November 2026 that Google has moved AI Overviews for many brand-related searches into the right-hand column of the results page. That is the spot where knowledge panels used to sit. PPC Land reported that every example Search Engine Roundtable published shows US results. Neither report says whether the layout appears in Australia. This post covers what was reported, what is still unknown, and a short checklist you can finish in one sitting.

By AIkonX10 November 2026

What was reported

  • 2 November 2026: Search Engine Roundtable (Barry Schwartz) reported that Google "has moved the AI Overviews for many brand-related keywords to the right-hand column of the search results page." It said they previously appeared at the top or middle of the main search results section.
  • Chris Long posted about it on X. Search Engine Roundtable quotes him: "Instead of a Knowledge Panel, they're showing AI Overviews for brand terms in the right column."
  • 31 October 2026: Search Engine Roundtable says David McSweeney spotted it a couple of days earlier. His post, dated 31 October, described AI Overviews for branded searches "generally in a right panel on desktop (or in some cases further down the page)." He also wrote that he was "seeing some other variations too."
  • Examples: PPC Land's report of 3 November 2026 says the published examples cover Apple, Zillow, Coca-Cola, Google and Search Engine Roundtable. For the first four, PPC Land says the brand's own website opens the main column and the AI summary sits to the side.
  • Barry Schwartz's view: he wrote that he thinks the new placement works better than the main results, but that "it is not great to trust that AI will do a good enough job writing about your brand than your own website would."

What is not confirmed

PPC Land reported that Google has not announced the change. It said no help page update or Google statement accompanies the Search Engine Roundtable report, and that Google has not said whether the layout is a test or permanent.

PPC Land also reported that every example Search Engine Roundtable published shows results set to the United States, so there is no evidence yet about other markets. It describes the rollout as uneven, and says none of the three people who noticed it published a count of how many brand searches are affected. It adds that Google publishes no click data for AI Overviews by placement, so the effect on visits to brand websites is unknown.

For Australia, the reports don't confirm availability. We have not verified the layout on Australian searches for this post, so please don't assume your results look like the examples. The checklist below shows how to look at your own.

Why it matters for an Australian business

The examples in the reports are large, well-known brands, and many Australian businesses are not household names. The practical question is the same for any business, though. When someone searches your business name, what does Google, or any AI tool, say about you?

PPC Land pointed to one detail in the Zillow example. The AI-written summary and the fact rows beside it gave two different founding years for the same company. PPC Land said each year had a basis, but the panel did not explain the gap. A small business can have the same problem on a smaller scale, such as an old address, a retired service or a previous phone number turning up in a summary.

This part is our reading, not Google's statement. If summaries like this appear for your brand search, they could shape a first impression before anyone clicks. Whether that happens, and how often, is unknown. Schwartz's concern is that the description is written by AI and not by your own website. AI answers can't be directly controlled. What you can do is keep the sources they draw on accurate and consistent. That is the focus of our AI search optimisation service, which structures your content and brand signals so AI tools can cite and recommend you.

What to check this week

  1. Search your brand name on desktop. Use a normal browser window and look at the right column and the main results. Note whether you see an AI Overview, a knowledge panel or neither.
  2. Search it on mobile too. The reports describe a desktop layout, and McSweeney's post says some summaries appeared further down the page. Neither report says how mobile looks, so note what you see on your own phone.
  3. Compare the summary with your own website. Check your services, locations, opening hours, phone number, founding details and ownership. Mark anything wrong, outdated or missing.
  4. Find which pages it cites. If the summary shows source links, open each one. Note whether they are your site, your Google Business Profile, a directory or a third-party page.
  5. Fix problems at the source. Update your website first. Then correct your Google Business Profile and any directory listings that carry old details. You can't edit an AI answer directly, so fix the pages it relies on.
  6. Keep business details consistent. Use the same name, address, phone number and service descriptions on your website, profile and directories.
  7. Record dated notes. Write down the date, device, location and what you saw. AI answers change, so a dated record lets you compare next month.

AI tools can give different answers to the same question depending on wording, location, device and timing. Our post on why AI visibility reports can disagree explains how to read those differences.

Questions

Has Google confirmed this change?

PPC Land reported that Google has not announced it, and that no Google statement accompanies the Search Engine Roundtable report.

Does it apply in Australia?

The reports don't confirm Australian availability. PPC Land says all the published examples show US results. Search your own brand on desktop to see what appears for you.

Is this a knowledge panel or an AI Overview?

Search Engine Roundtable reported that AI Overviews are being shown in the right column for brand terms in place of the knowledge panel. Schwartz wrote that Google is "essentially replacing brand knowledge panels with AI Overviews."

Can I edit what the AI Overview says about my business?

Not directly. PPC Land reports that Google has not explained whether the "Suggest edits" option on a panel extends to the AI-written text. The practical step is to correct the sources your business details come from.

Will this change how many people click my website?

Unknown. PPC Land reports that Google publishes no click data for AI Overviews by placement.
