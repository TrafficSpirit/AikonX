How to Run an AI Visibility Audit (Checklist)

An AI visibility audit is a dated record of what AI tools say when someone asks about your kind of business. You write a fixed set of questions, run them through ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, and note whether you are mentioned, cited or left out. Then you repeat the check on later days and compare.

By Anisa Chong

You don't need a specialist tool to start. A spreadsheet and a signed-out browser window will do. No audit can promise that an AI tool will name your business. It shows where you stand and whether anything changes.

What is an AI visibility audit?

AI visibility is how often, and how accurately, AI tools bring up your business when people ask relevant questions. An audit measures it. It records whether your brand is mentioned, whether your website is linked as a source, which competitors appear and how your business is described.

This is the same ground that AI visibility reports cover. The difference is who runs it. A report is often produced by a tool. An audit is a process you follow yourself, so you know what was asked, where and when.

An audit is a sample of answers under particular conditions. It is not a ranking like a position on Google. Change the wording, the location or the day and the answer can change.

Our guide to getting cited in Google AI Overviews covers one platform and the page fixes that go with it. This post doesn't repeat that checklist. It covers the measuring, across several tools, before and after any fixing.

Step 1: Which questions go in your prompt set?

Your prompt set is the list of questions you ask every tool.

  1. Write 15 questions your customers really ask. Use their words, not your industry's. A dentist might test "emergency dentist Prahran Saturday" and "how much does a crown cost in Melbourne".
  2. Include your suburb or service area. Answers to local questions can depend on location.
  3. Mix the types. Add "who should I call" questions, cost questions and "best way to" questions. Include two or three that name your business.
  4. Lock the wording and number each prompt. If you change a question, record it as a new one.

Fifteen questions across four tools is 60 checks for round one. The repeat rounds in Step 3 multiply that, so resist starting with a longer list.

Step 2: How do you set up a fair test on each platform?

Run the audit the same way every time. Our reports guide lists what can change an answer: prompt wording, platform and mode, location, account and history, and timing.

  • Use a signed-out or private window where the tool allows it.
  • Use your normal location and note it on the sheet.
  • Record the platform, the mode or model if you can see it, the device and the date.
  • Use the live, public version of each tool.
  • Expect gaps on Google. Our AI Overviews guide summarises Google's documentation as saying AI Overviews are only shown when its systems decide they add something beyond classic Search. A blank result is a real result, so write it down.

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews are separate products. Treat each as its own measurement, and don't assume one tool's behaviour applies to another.

Step 3: Why run each prompt more than once?

Ask the same question twice and you may get two different replies, naming different businesses. A single run can mislead you in either direction. You might look invisible when you are sometimes present, or look strong because you caught a lucky answer.

A simple approach:

  • Round one: run all 15 prompts on all four platforms (60 checks).
  • Rounds two and three: repeat the same 60 checks on two later days, in fresh sessions.
  • Log every run on its own row. That is 180 runs in total.
  • Score each prompt by how many of its three runs included you, such as 2 out of 3.

Three runs is our suggestion for a small business, not a standard from any platform. If 180 runs is too many, cut prompts rather than repeats. The repeats show how stable your presence is.

Step 4: How do you track mentions and citations?

A mention, a citation and a recommendation are different outcomes, as our earlier guides explain:

  • Mention: the AI names your brand somewhere in the answer.
  • Citation: the AI links to your website, or a page about you, as a source.
  • Recommendation: the AI presents you as an option worth choosing.

A business can be mentioned often and cited rarely. It can be cited for a fact without being recommended. If you merge these into one tick, you lose the detail that shows what to work on.

For each run, record whether you were mentioned, cited (and which page was linked) and recommended. Add a short note on how you were described, and flag any wrong detail such as an old address, a retired service or an outdated phone number.

Open the sources each answer links to. Are they business websites, directories, Business Profile listings or news sites? That shows which kinds of page appeared for that question. It doesn't tell you why they were chosen, so don't read it as a ranking formula.

Step 5: How do you compare yourself with competitors?

Each time an answer names a business, add it to a competitor tally. You don't need to decide in advance who your competitors are. The tools will show you who they associate with your questions, and it is sometimes a surprise. It may be a directory or comparison site rather than another local business.

For each prompt, note which businesses were named, which were cited with a link, and whether the same names keep appearing across platforms. At the end, count how often each name appears. You are looking for patterns, not precision.

If one competitor appears in most runs and you appear in few, compare how their service pages answer the question with how yours do. Don't copy them. Work out what you can say that is true and specific to your business.

Step 6: What does a scoring sheet look like?

Here is a simple sheet for a spreadsheet. It is our own method for keeping audits comparable, not an industry standard.

Raw log columns: date, prompt number, platform, run number, location, mentioned (Y/N), cited (Y/N), recommended (Y/N), cited page, competitors named, description note, errors spotted.

Summary, one row per platform:

  • Prompts where you were mentioned in at least one run
  • Prompts where you were cited in at least one run
  • Prompts where you were recommended in at least one run
  • Runs with an error in how you were described
  • Top three competitors by count

A simple score: give one point for each mention, two for each citation and three for each recommendation, then divide by the number of runs. The weighting is a judgement call. Keep it the same between rounds or the comparison means nothing.

On the Google rows, add a "no AI Overview shown" column so blanks aren't mistaken for misses.

What should you do with the results?

Sort your findings into three piles:

  1. Errors. Wrong or outdated business details. Fix these first, on your website, your Business Profile and your directory listings.
  2. Gaps. Questions where you never appear. Check whether you have a page that answers each one plainly.
  3. Strengths. Questions where you appear reliably. Note what those pages do well.

Write down the date of every change so the next audit has something to compare against. Then repeat the audit on a fixed schedule with the same prompts, platforms and settings. Monthly suits many small businesses, and quarterly is reasonable if little moves.

When something changes, avoid jumping to a cause. AI tools update often, and the sources they draw on keep changing. A rise or fall may reflect your work, a competitor's, or neither. Read the audit alongside your search, analytics and enquiry data. It doesn't prove that an AI answer led to a call or a sale.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI visibility audit?

An AI visibility audit is a dated record of what AI tools say about your business. You run a fixed set of questions through tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, and record mentions, citations and competitors.

How many prompts do I need?

Start with about 15 questions your customers really ask, including your suburb or service area. Keep the list fixed so each round can be compared with the last. That number is our suggestion, not a platform rule.

Why run each prompt more than once?

AI answers vary from one run to the next. Running each prompt on several days, in separate sessions, shows whether you appear reliably or only sometimes. We suggest three runs per platform.

How often should I repeat the audit?

Monthly suits many small businesses, and quarterly is reasonable if little changes. Use the same prompts, platforms and settings each time, and keep a dated record.

Can an audit guarantee my business will be cited?

No. An audit measures what appeared in a sample of answers. AI tools decide their own answers and sources, and those change over time, so nobody can guarantee a mention or citation.
