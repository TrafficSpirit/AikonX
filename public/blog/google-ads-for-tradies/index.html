Google Ads for Tradies: Budgets, Keywords and Common Mistakes

Google Ads for tradies works when your ads show for urgent, local searches, your budget matches what a job is worth, and you can see which ads make the phone ring. It fails when clicks are wasted on the wrong searches and nobody counts the calls. This guide covers Search ads and Local Services Ads, how to size a budget from your own numbers, which keywords to exclude, and why call tracking decides whether any of it pays off.

By Anisa Chong

How do Google Ads for tradies work?

You pay Google when someone clicks your ad after searching for something like "emergency plumber Brunswick" or "electrician near me". Our tradies page describes these searches as coming from people with a problem to fix now, often on a phone and in a hurry.

That is the main reason ads suit trades. Ads can put you in front of that person today, while organic work takes longer to build. The trade-off is that ads stop the moment you stop paying. Many tradies run both: ads for immediate enquiries and SEO for long-term growth, as we explain on the tradies page.

If you want us to run this for you, see our Google Ads management. The rest of this post is what to understand before you spend anything.

Search ads or Local Services Ads: which suits a tradie?

Search ads are the standard Google Ads format. You choose keywords, write the ad, set a budget and pay per click. They run in Australia today.

Local Services Ads are a different product. According to our Local Services Ads page, they appear above regular search ads for many service categories, show your reviews and a Google verification badge, let people call or message you directly, and charge only when a genuine lead comes through.

They are not available to Australian businesses. Search Scope, an Australian SEO agency, reports that Google's Local Services sign-up lists Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States, and that Australia is not on the list. Search Scope also says that anyone offering to set up Local Services Ads for an Australian business is selling something that does not exist here yet.

So for a tradie in Melbourne, Search ads are the format to plan around. Reviews and fast phone answering help in any case, because they support your Google Business Profile and your ads alike.

How much should a tradie spend on Google Ads?

We won't give you a "typical" budget, because it depends on your suburb, your trade and what competitors bid. Work backwards from your own numbers instead.

Here is the method, with made-up figures to show the arithmetic. These are not benchmarks and not a forecast.

  1. Cost per click. Suppose a click costs $10.
  2. Click-to-call rate. Suppose 1 click in 10 becomes a phone call or form. That is $100 per enquiry.
  3. Enquiry-to-job rate. Suppose 1 enquiry in 3 becomes a booked job. That is $300 per job.
  4. What a job is worth. If a job leaves you $600 after materials and labour, you can see whether $300 to win it makes sense. If it leaves $250, it does not.

Your real numbers will differ. Until you have your own data, treat each figure as an unknown and test a range. Then decide a monthly spend you can afford to run for long enough to learn something, and set daily budgets to match.

For context on what else tradies pay for leads, Search Scope reports that hipages plans run from $129 + GST to $599 + GST per month, with credits spent on each lead on top, and that up to three tradies can accept the same lead. Ads give you the customer's search, not a shared lead, but you carry the click costs and the management either way.

If you hire a provider, ask what the fee covers, whether it is GST inclusive, and whether you own the ad account.

Which keywords work for Google Ads for plumbers and other trades?

Start with the searches that signal a job: the service plus a place. Examples:

  • "blocked drain plumber Coburg"
  • "emergency electrician Melbourne"
  • "hot water system replacement"
  • "roof repair [suburb]"

Google Ads for plumbers usually works best when each service gets its own ad group and its own landing page. A search for "gas fitter" should land on a page about gas fitting, with your phone number visible, not on your home page.

Keep the list tight. A few specific services in the suburbs you actually travel to will beat a long list of broad words. Broad terms bring clicks from people who want a free tip, a job application or a plumber three hours away.

What are negative keywords, and why do they matter?

Negative keywords tell Google which searches should not trigger your ads. For a plumber, that might be "jobs", "apprenticeship", "course", "diy" or "salary". Each blocked search is a click you do not pay for.

The details matter, and they catch people out. MarketinGO, a Google Ads agency, quotes Google Ads Help on how negatives behave:

  • Negative keywords do not match close variants. Block "recruiter" and searches for "recruiters" can still show your ad. Add plurals and spellings yourself.
  • A negative typed with no punctuation is negative broad by default. A single word like "free" blocks every search that contains it.
  • Google can ignore a negative that sits after the sixteenth word of a very long search.
  • A negative that overlaps with a keyword you bid on can stop that keyword showing at all.

So don't paste in a giant downloaded list. Read your search terms report every week for the first month, add the wasted searches you actually see, and use the narrowest match type that works. Check you are not blocking a search that books work. "Jobs" is a good negative for a tradie, but "free quote" might be a search you want to win.

Why does call tracking matter for Google Ads?

For many businesses, the best leads call rather than fill in a form. Without call tracking, those calls are invisible in your analytics, so ads that produce phone enquiries look like they are underperforming. That is how good campaigns get paused.

Our call tracking service uses dynamic number insertion, so each visitor sees a number based on how they arrived. Calls forward to your existing line, and the calls are sent to Google Ads and GA4 as conversions, so bidding can learn from real enquiries. Done properly, your main number stays consistent on your Google Business Profile and directory listings, so local SEO is not affected.

The result is a report that says which keyword made the phone ring, not just which one got a click. That is the number you use to adjust budgets.

What are the most common Google Ads mistakes tradies make?

  • Counting clicks instead of jobs. Cost per click is not cost per job. Track calls and quotes.
  • Sending every ad to the home page. Match each ad to a page about that service.
  • Targeting the whole of Melbourne. Limit ads to the suburbs you will drive to.
  • Skipping negative keywords. Check the search terms report weekly.
  • Running ads at hours nobody answers. A missed call is a wasted click. Match ad times to when you pick up, or use a call-back service.
  • Not owning the account. If a provider leaves, you should keep the account, data and creative.
  • Setting a budget too small to learn from. Two clicks a day teaches you nothing.

Where do ads fit with the rest of your digital marketing?

Digital marketing for tradies is usually a mix. Ads buy immediate visibility. A complete Google Business Profile, reviews and local pages build the free listings that keep working when the budget stops. Ads are the faster tool, so use them for a quiet fortnight or a new suburb, and treat the organic side as the asset you own.

Frequently asked questions

Are Google Ads worth it for a one-person trade business?

Often, if a single job is worth enough to cover the cost of winning it. Run the budget arithmetic above with your own numbers. If the maths doesn't work, SEO or referrals may be a better first step.

Are Local Services Ads available in Australia?

No. Search Scope reports that Google's Local Services sign-up does not list Australia. Standard Google Ads, paired with a strong Google Business Profile, is the option that works here today.

How long before Google Ads bring in calls?

Ads can start showing the day they are approved, so calls can arrive in days. The campaign usually improves as you add negative keywords and learn which searches convert. Nobody can honestly promise a number of leads.

Do I need call tracking if I already get calls?

Yes, if you want to know which ads caused them. Without it, you can't tell a winning keyword from a wasted one.
