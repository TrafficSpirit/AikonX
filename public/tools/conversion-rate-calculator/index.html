How to calculate conversion rate

Conversion rate is the share of visitors who complete the action you want, such as submitting an enquiry form, calling, booking or buying.

Conversion rate (%) = (Conversions ÷ Total visitors) × 100

Worked example: a landing page receives 2,000 visitors in a month and generates 50 enquiries. 50 ÷ 2,000 = 0.025, × 100 = a 2.5% conversion rate.

Use the same date range and the same definition of a visitor (users or sessions) for both numbers, and count one type of conversion at a time so you know exactly what you are measuring.

What is a good conversion rate?

It depends on the channel, the offer and the goal. As a rough guide for Australian businesses, lead generation landing pages commonly convert at 2–5%, top-performing pages can reach 10% or more, and e-commerce checkout pages typically convert at 1–3%. Your own trend over time is the most useful benchmark.

How to improve your conversion rate

  • Find where visitors drop off, using analytics funnels, heatmaps and session recordings
  • Match the headline and offer to the ad or search that brought the visitor
  • Give the page one clear call to action and remove distractions around it
  • Shorten forms to the fields you genuinely need
  • Add proof near the decision point: reviews, guarantees, credentials
  • Check page speed and the mobile layout
  • A/B test changes rather than relying on opinion

Questions, answered

What is a good conversion rate for a website?

A good website conversion rate depends on the channel and goal. For lead generation landing pages in Australia, 2–5% is a reasonable benchmark. Top-performing pages can reach 10%+. E-commerce checkout pages typically convert at 1–3%.

What is the formula for conversion rate?

Conversion Rate (%) = (Conversions ÷ Total Visitors) × 100. For example, 50 leads from 2,000 visitors gives a 2.5% conversion rate.

How do I improve my conversion rate?

Start with a CRO audit: analyse where visitors drop off using heatmaps and session recordings, then run A/B tests on headlines, CTAs, forms and page flow. Even small improvements compound across every traffic channel.

What is the difference between conversion rate and click-through rate?

CTR measures the share of people who clicked an ad or link. Conversion rate measures the share of visitors who completed a desired action on a page (e.g. submitted a form or purchased). CTR happens before the landing; conversion rate happens after.
