How to calculate ROAS

Return on ad spend tells you how much revenue your advertising generates for every dollar you put in. It is the quickest way to compare campaigns, platforms and audiences on the same scale.

ROAS = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend

Worked example: a Google Shopping campaign spends $5,000 in a month and drives $20,000 in tracked sales. $20,000 ÷ $5,000 = a ROAS of 4, also written as 4:1, 4x or 400%.

Find your break-even ROAS

A ROAS figure only means something next to your margins. Your break-even ROAS is the point where gross profit from ad-driven sales exactly covers the ad spend:

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross margin

With a 40% gross margin, break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5. A campaign at ROAS 4 is profitable for that business; the same ROAS 4 would lose money for a business with a 20% margin, whose break-even ROAS is 5.

How to improve ROAS

  • Make sure conversion tracking records real sales and leads, with values
  • Cut search terms, placements and audiences that spend without converting
  • Shift budget towards your highest-margin products and services
  • Use bidding strategies that optimise for conversion value, not clicks
  • Improve landing page conversion rate so every click is worth more
  • Refresh ad creative before performance fades

Questions, answered

What is ROAS?

ROAS (return on ad spend) is the revenue generated for every dollar spent on advertising. A ROAS of 4 means each $1 of ad spend returned $4 in revenue.

What is the formula for ROAS?

ROAS = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend. For example, $20,000 in revenue from $5,000 of ad spend gives a ROAS of 4 (often written 4:1, 4x or 400%).

What is a good ROAS?

There is no universal number because it depends on your margins. Work out your break-even ROAS by dividing 1 by your gross margin: with a 40% margin, break-even ROAS is 2.5. Anything above break-even is profitable before other overheads.

What is the difference between ROAS and ROI?

ROAS compares revenue with ad spend only. ROI compares profit with the total investment, including product costs, fees and management. A campaign can have a healthy-looking ROAS and still lose money if margins are thin.
