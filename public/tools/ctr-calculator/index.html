How to calculate CTR

Click-through rate is the share of people who saw your ad, email or search listing and clicked it. It is an early signal of how relevant your message is to the audience seeing it.

CTR (%) = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100

Worked example: a search ad is shown 5,000 times and clicked 150 times. 150 ÷ 5,000 = 0.03, × 100 = a 3% CTR. If that cost $600, the cost per click is $600 ÷ 150 = $4.

What is a good CTR?

There is no single number. CTR varies by network, industry, ad position and whether the search includes your brand name, and search ads usually earn higher CTRs than display or social ads. Compare against your own baseline for the same campaign type and watch the trend over time.

How to improve CTR

  • Tighten targeting so ads show to people searching for what you actually sell
  • Add negative keywords to stop irrelevant impressions
  • Put the searcher's words and your strongest benefit in the headline
  • Use every relevant asset: sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets and calls
  • Test new hooks and creative regularly, especially on social
  • For organic results, write title tags and meta descriptions that answer the query

Questions, answered

What is click-through rate (CTR)?

CTR is the percentage of people who saw your ad, email or search listing and clicked it. It shows how relevant and compelling your message is to the people seeing it.

What is the formula for CTR?

CTR (%) = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100. For example, 150 clicks from 5,000 impressions gives a 3% CTR.

What is a good CTR?

It depends on the network, industry, ad position and whether the keyword includes your brand. Search ads usually earn higher CTRs than display or social ads. The most useful benchmark is your own baseline for the same campaign type, tracked over time.

Is a higher CTR always better?

Not on its own. Clicks that do not convert still cost money. Judge CTR alongside conversion rate and ROAS so you attract the right clicks, not just more of them.
